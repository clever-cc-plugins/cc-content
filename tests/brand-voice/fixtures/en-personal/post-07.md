I used to think saying no to a client was a failure. Turns out it's a service.

Last spring a client asked me to add a chatbot to their app. I asked why. "Everybody has one." Fair enough, but I looked at their support tickets, and 70 percent of them were the same question: where's my invoice? A chatbot would've answered that question very expensively. A button labelled "Download invoice" would've answered it for free.

I told them so. They were unhappy for about a day. Then they built the button, and the tickets vanished. They're now my best referral source, which still surprises me.

There's a lesson here, and I'd rather you find it than have me spell it out. Okay, fine, I'll spell it out: the job isn't to do what's asked. It's to figure out what's needed, and be brave enough to say so.

What's the best "no" you ever gave?

And yes, sometimes the client insists. Then I do the thing, quote it honestly, and write down my concern. That's not stubbornness. It's keeping a record I can point to when we revisit the decision in six months.
