I got feedback last week that stung. A client said, "Your work is great, but you explain too much."

Ouch. And, annoyingly, fair. I'd written a 14-page rationale for a four-screen prototype. Nobody asked for it. I wanted them to understand my thinking, and I ended up burying the thinking under paragraphs.

So I tried something different this week. Four screens, one page of notes, and a voice memo for anyone who wanted the long version. Guess how many people listened to the memo? Two. Out of nine. And both of them were my own team.

Here's what I'm taking from this: confidence isn't explaining everything. It's trusting the work to carry most of the weight, and being ready to talk when someone asks.

Have you ever over-explained something because you were nervous? I'm raising my hand.

I'm not abandoning documentation, by the way. I just changed who it's for. It's now a reference for the team, not a speech for the client. Different audience, different tone, and, weirdly, much less stress on my end.
