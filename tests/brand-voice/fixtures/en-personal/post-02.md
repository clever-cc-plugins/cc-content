Hot take: your onboarding doesn't need a progress bar. It needs fewer steps.

I know, I know. Progress bars feel like a clever fix. Users see "Step 2 of 7" and, in theory, feel motivated. In practice, they see "5 more things I have to do" and quietly close the tab.

I watched this happen last month in a usability session. A woman got to step 3, stared at the bar for a good five seconds, and said, "Oh, there's more?" She wasn't angry. She was just done.

We cut the flow from seven steps to three. Everything we removed was something we could ask later, once she'd actually seen the product work. Completion went up by a third. Nobody missed the progress bar, including me.

If you can't remove a step, can you at least move it? I'd love to hear what you've tried.

A caveat, because I can already hear the replies: some products really do need long setup. Banking, health, anything regulated. Fine. But even there, I'd bet half the steps exist because somebody once said "we might need it." Might. Not do.
