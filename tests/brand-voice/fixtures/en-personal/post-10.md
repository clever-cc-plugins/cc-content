Nobody tells you how much of design is just deleting things.

I started out thinking the job was making. New screens, new components, new flows. Years in, I'd say about half my best work is subtraction. The settings page with thirty options that became nine. The email with four calls to action that became one. The dashboard that showed everything and told nobody anything.

Deleting is harder than adding, though. Every element has an owner, and every owner has a reason. "But what if somebody needs it?" is the most expensive sentence in product work. I've heard it so often I could set it to music.

My trick is small: before I cut anything, I ask for the data on who uses it. Usually the answer is "nobody," or "a few people who'd be fine without it." Occasionally it's "our biggest customer," and then the thing stays. That's okay. At least we chose.
