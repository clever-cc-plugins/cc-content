I've sat through 200+ design reviews. Exactly three changed a decision.

The rest were theatre. Someone shares a screen, someone says "love the direction," and everyone goes back to doing what they were already going to do. I'm not above this. I've run a few of those reviews myself, and I'm not proud of it.

Here's what the three useful ones had in common: somebody walked in with a question they were genuinely afraid to ask. "Does this flow make sense to a first-time user, or are we just used to it?" That kind of question. Uncomfortable, specific, answerable.

So now I open every review the same way. I write one sentence at the top of the doc: "The decision we need today is…" If I can't finish that sentence, I cancel the meeting. My calendar's never been healthier.

What's the last review you left feeling like something actually got decided?

P.S. A friend asked whether this kills spontaneity. Honestly? I haven't noticed. The good conversations still happen, they just start with a clearer premise. And the bad ones end ten minutes sooner, which my future self thanks me for.
