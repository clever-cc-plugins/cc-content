Three words I banned from my client decks this year: "synergy," "seamless," and "journey."

Not because they're evil. Because they're lazy. When I write "seamless experience," I'm skipping the hard part, which is explaining what actually happens when the user taps the button and the network drops. Spoiler: it isn't seamless. It's a spinner and a prayer.

So I replaced them with plain descriptions. "Seamless checkout" became "checkout that saves your cart if the connection drops." Longer? Yes. Better? Ask any client who's had to sign it off. They actually know what they're buying.

My editor friend says this is just "show, don't tell" for slides. Maybe. I think it's simpler: if I can't describe it, I haven't designed it yet.

Which buzzword would you ban first?

Funny side effect: my decks got shorter. When you can't hide behind a word like "holistic," you notice how little you had to say on that slide. I deleted eleven of them. Nobody asked where they went.
