A quick reminder that "user-friendly" isn't a feature. It's a result.

I get briefs all the time that list it between "scalable" and "modern." As if you can sprinkle it on at the end. You can't. It's what you get when someone has spent a long time watching real people struggle with the thing, and then fixed it.

The best example I know is a boring one: a tax form. The team behind it didn't redesign the interface at all. They sat in a room and listened to forty phone calls from confused users. Then they rewrote six questions. Support calls dropped by half. That's it. That's the whole redesign.

So the next time a brief says "user-friendly," I ask one thing: "Who have we watched?" Sometimes the room goes quiet.

Who have you watched this week?

I'll add this: watching is uncomfortable. You see people fail at something you thought was obvious, and your first instinct is to explain. Don't. Take notes. Come back to the explanation later, when you're less defensive.
