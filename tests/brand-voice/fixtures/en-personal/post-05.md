We're running a tiny experiment at my studio: no laptops in the first ten minutes of any meeting.

I know how that sounds. Very "wellness retreat." I resisted it for months. But here's what happened. In the first meeting, we spent ten minutes just talking about what the problem was. No slides, no Figma, no half-typed Slack replies. At the end, we realised we'd been solving the wrong problem for two weeks. Two weeks!

We haven't made it a rule. It's more of a habit we're trying on. Some meetings don't need it, and a few people hate it, which is fine. I don't want it to turn into a ritual nobody can explain.

But I keep thinking about those ten minutes. What did we skip before, when everyone was typing?

Do you have a meeting habit you'd defend to the end?

If you try this, don't announce it as a policy. Just start the meeting by closing your own laptop and see what happens. People usually follow within a minute. Nobody likes being the only one typing, it turns out.
